The problem

Before someone calls, they may search your name. Patients, clients, referral partners, boards and journalists can all look, and you may never hear what they found.

  • Old or one-sided results

    A complaint, a lawsuit mention or a news story from years ago can still sit near the top, with nothing beside it to give context.

  • Someone else with your name

    Results about another person can be read as yours, including another doctor or lawyer in a different state.

  • Directories that disagree

    Old addresses, wrong specialties and past employers repeat across listing sites you never set up yourself.

  • A thin presence

    Decades of work can come down to one outdated staff page, which leaves room for whatever else appears.

  • Reviews without context

    When little else is published about you, a few strong opinions can do most of the talking.

  • AI answers that miss

    People now ask ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini about you. They may know nothing, repeat an old role, or get a detail wrong.

Negative reviews and bad press

A negative review is not always about the quality of the work. Knowing what may be behind one helps decide what to do.

  • One hard moment

    A long wait, a billing surprise or a difficult conversation can lead to a review, even when the work itself was sound.

  • Outcomes nobody controls

    A lost case or a treatment that did not work can lead to a review about the result, not the care or the advice.

  • You cannot answer in detail

    Privacy and confidentiality rules mean the professional side of the story usually cannot be told.

  • Little else to read

    When there is little accurate material about you, one review or one old story does most of the talking.

What to do when a bad review or bad press appears

  1. Do not reply in anger. A heated or detailed reply can do more harm than the review, and can break privacy rules.
  2. Check whether it breaks the site rules. Fake, abusive or conflicted reviews can be reported. The site decides.
  3. Reply briefly where appropriate. A short, general reply that invites the person to call the office shows care without revealing anything.
  4. Correct what is wrong. Wrong facts in a news story or listing can be raised with the source, with your documents.
  5. Add accurate material over time. A steady run of approved, factual content about your work gives searchers more to read than one complaint.

What we do

The same six steps for every client, in this order.

  1. 1

    Baseline

    We run a fixed list of searches for your name and field, ask the main AI assistants about you, and save it all with the date, so change can be measured later.

  2. 2

    Audit

    We list every source, what it says about you and whether it is right. You get written findings, most important first.

  3. 3

    Agree

    You approve one version of your facts and a plan. Nothing is sent or published without your approval.

  4. 4

    Correct

    We update the profiles you control and send correction requests to the ones you do not.

  5. 5

    Publish

    We prepare accurate material with you. A press release can take a place in the results for your name, but it does not raise your rankings by itself.

  6. 6

    Review

    We run the same list again, show you what moved and what did not, and you decide what comes next.

You decide whether the work continues.

Three places your reputation lives

Each one works differently, so each needs different work.

  • Search engines

    What we do

    We improve the pages you own, make them easy for search engines to read, and look for real mentions on other sites.

    How it works

    They rank pages by relevance and by how much other sites trust them. Nobody can buy a place in the unpaid results.

  • Review and directory sites

    What we do

    We claim profiles where the site allows it, correct them, and make every one state the same facts.

    How it works

    Many build listings on their own from public data, so one error can spread to many sites.

  • AI assistants

    What we do

    We ask them about you, save the answers, correct the sources they rely on, and ask again later.

    How it works

    They draw on the open web and search indexes. There is no form to submit. They describe you with more confidence when the same facts appear across several sources, including at least one that is not you.

What it looks like in your field

The sites and the rules differ by profession. We work within yours.

Physicians
Where people look
Google, Healthgrades, Doximity, Vitals, hospital staff pages and state medical board lookups.
What often goes wrong
Old hospital affiliations, a wrong specialty, an outdated office address, reviews with no reply.
The rule we work within
Patient privacy law applies to public replies. A reply must not reveal a patient's details, so we keep replies general enough not to confirm someone was your patient. Your practice approves every reply.
Attorneys
Where people look
Google, state bar profiles, Avvo, Justia and your firm biography.
What often goes wrong
An old firm name, wrong practice areas, an attorney with the same name, unanswered reviews.
The rule we work within
Confidentiality duties limit what a lawyer can say when answering online criticism, and advertising rules vary by state. You stay responsible for compliance, and we do not give legal advice.
Executives and founders
Where people look
Google, LinkedIn, company leadership pages, Crunchbase and the trade press.
What often goes wrong
A past role shown as current, old news with no follow up, little about your recent work.
The rule we work within
Anything touching a company, its investors or a legal matter goes through your counsel or communications team first.

What can change, and what cannot

Knowing this first saves time, money and false hope.

  • Yours to change

    Usually quick, because you control it.

    • Your own website
    • LinkedIn and profiles you can claim
    • Your biography and how your credentials are stated
    • What your pages tell search engines

  • Can be requested

    We ask, with your documents. The site decides.

    • Corrections on directories and listing sites
    • An update from a publisher when the story moved on
    • Removal of a review that breaks the site's own rules
    • Removal of personal contact details through search engine tools, such as Google's Results about you

  • Not ours to decide

    Here we add accurate context around it.

    • Accurate news reports
    • Reviews that follow the site's rules
    • Court and public records
    • How search engines rank, and what an AI assistant says

When a result is negative

We ask three questions, in this order. The answers decide the work.

  1. 1Is it really about you?

    If not: mistaken identity

    We make you easier to tell apart: the same full name, credentials, city, field and photo everywhere.

  2. 2Is it accurate?

    If not: false or wrong

    We ask the source to correct it, with your documents. If it may be defamatory, we prepare the record and hand it to your attorney.

  3. 3Is it still current?

    If not: out of date

    We ask the publisher to update it, and publish the later outcome where you can document it.

  4. Accurate, current and about you?

    We do not try to hide it. We help you reply where a reply is appropriate, and add accurate material about the rest of your work, so it is not the only thing people read.

The ongoing program

One release on its own rarely changes much. The work that matters is steady, accurate material published over months, with your approval at every step.

  • A full first release

    Built with you from day one. You help shape the story, and we write it in detail from your documents.

  • Ongoing releases and content

    Further approved releases and articles on a set schedule, each about something true and current in your work.

  • Your website and profiles

    Your site and the profiles you control are kept accurate and connected, so new material links together.

  • Watching and reporting

    The same searches run each month, with a report on what changed and what we did.

What to expect over time

A typical order of work, not a promise. Timing depends on the sites involved, your documents and how many others share your name.

  1. First weeks

    Baseline, audit and your approved plan. Profiles you control can usually be corrected right away.

  2. Months one to three

    Correction requests go out, and each site answers on its own timing. The first approved material is published.

  3. Months three to six

    Search engines and AI assistants read the changes on their own schedule. We run the same searches again and report.

  4. After six months

    Your name usually moves first. Wider searches take far longer. From here the work is steady watching and new material.

How we handle reviews

Four rules, for every client.

  • No fake reviews, ever

    We never write, buy or arrange reviews. Federal rules ban fake reviews, and they would damage the thing you are trying to protect.

  • Ask real clients fairly

    Where your professional rules allow it, we help you ask real clients, without steering only the happy ones to a review site.

  • Calm, general replies

    A reply stays short and polite, and never confirms that someone was a patient or client. You approve it first.

  • Report only what breaks the rules

    A review that breaks the site's own rules can be reported, and the site decides. We do not try to remove a review just because it is unfavorable.

Questions people ask

Can you remove a bad review or article?

Not on demand. A review that breaks a site's rules can be reported, and a publisher can be asked to correct or update a story, but the site decides. Anyone promising removal is promising something they do not control.

Will I reach the first page of Google?

We do not promise positions. A search for your own name is usually where change is most realistic, because few pages compete for it. Wider searches, such as a specialty in a city, are much harder and depend heavily on links from other sites.

How long does this take?

What you control can change in days. Everything else depends on other sites and on search engines, and often takes months. We run the same searches again so you can see what moved.

Can you change what ChatGPT says about me?

Not directly, and nobody can. We correct the sources AI assistants read and make your facts agree across them, then ask again later. They update on their own schedule.

What if the results are about someone with my name?

It happens, and it can often be addressed. We state your full name, credentials, city and field the same way everywhere, so people and machines can tell the two of you apart.

What if something false is published about me?

We gather the record and ask the source to correct it. If it may be defamatory, that is a matter for your attorney. We are not a law firm.

Do I have to buy publishing as well?

No. Reputation management is a separate service. It does not put you in the registry, and it does not decide what a registry profile says.

Will you publish anything without asking me?

No. You approve the facts, the plan and every piece of content or reply before it goes out.

How do I stop?

The terms, including how to cancel, are given to you in writing before you enroll.
