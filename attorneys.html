For attorneys

Clients and referral sources research a lawyer before they call. We turn your admissions, practice history and published work into one accurate record, approved by you.

The problem

Most attorneys have a firm biography, a bar listing and a scatter of directory pages. Together they rarely explain what you do, where you have done it, or what you have written and taught.

  • A thin firm biography

    A paragraph on the firm site, written years ago, that lists a school and a bar admission and stops there.

  • A practice history in pieces

    Earlier firms, government service, clerkships and moves between practice areas are scattered or missing.

  • Articles nobody can find

    Bar journal pieces, CLE talks and published decisions live in places clients never look.

  • Outdated public information

    Old firm names, past addresses and practice areas you no longer handle repeat across directories.

  • Confusion with another lawyer

    Another attorney with a similar name can appear first, sometimes in another state.

  • Experience that is hard to explain

    Clients cannot tell what you actually do day to day, or whether you handle matters like theirs.

Why it matters

The decision to call your office often starts with a search for your name.

  1. A client is referred to you

    Even with a strong referral, most people search the lawyer before they call.

  2. They compare what they find

    Firm pages, bar profiles, directories and reviews sit side by side on one screen.

  3. Referral sources check too

    Other lawyers, accountants and advisers look at your background before sending a matter.

  4. Clear experience earns the call

    A complete, accurate account of your practice makes the decision easier.

The solution: one record, in the forms people use

We write one accurate account of your career from your documents, you approve it, and we publish it where people look: a website under your own name, a registry profile, a press release and, where your package includes them, printed pieces. Every version says the same thing, so a search for your name leads to a clear and consistent picture.

What it looks like by practice area

Every field has its own blind spots. These are typical, not promises about any one person.

  • Personal injury attorneys

    Common gap
    Ads and listing sites crowd the results, and your own record of practice, firm history and community work is hard to see.
    What we do
    A site under your name and a biography set out your practice areas, background and service factually, without verdict claims.

  • Criminal defense attorneys

    Common gap
    Prospective clients act fast and judge on very little. Former prosecution or public defender experience is often left out.
    What we do
    We document your background, courts and practice focus clearly, within your state's advertising rules.

  • Estate planning attorneys

    Common gap
    Families choose carefully and slowly, but your approach, credentials and published guidance are spread out or missing.
    What we do
    Your biography explains your focus, training and the kinds of planning you handle, linked to anything you have published.

  • Business and corporate counsel

    Common gap
    In-house roles, transactions and board service rarely appear in public, so your experience looks smaller than it is.
    What we do
    We describe roles and responsibilities you can document and approve for publication.

  • Family law attorneys

    Common gap
    Sensitive work means little can be said about matters, and clients see only a name and a firm.
    What we do
    We focus on your training, approach and service, never on clients or cases.

  • Solo and small firm lawyers

    Common gap
    There is no marketing department, and the firm site is the only page about you.
    What we do
    A personal website and registry profile give you more than one accurate page to share.

What your profile and biography cover

  • Current firm, position and city
  • Practice areas, in words your state allows
  • Bar admissions and courts, from your records
  • Law school, degrees and clerkships
  • Earlier firms, government and in-house roles
  • Articles, books, talks and CLE teaching
  • Bar association, pro bono and community service
  • Recognition from other bodies, with the source
  • A professional photo you provide or approve
  • The sources behind each fact

What we will not write

  • Results and case outcomes. No verdicts, settlements or win claims, and nothing that implies a result can be repeated.
  • Specialist claims. Many states limit when a lawyer may be called a specialist or expert. We use the wording your rules allow.
  • Client information. Nothing about any client or matter, and no client testimonials.
  • Paid recognition as an honor. Any paid award or certificate is labeled as part of a paid service, for clients who meet eligibility.

How publishing works

  1. 1Information

    You send your CV, licenses or bar details, education, positions, publications and a photo. A short call fills the gaps.

  2. 2Research and drafting

    We check your details against your documents and public sources, then write the biography, release and site pages.

  3. 3Your review

    You read every word. Mark anything that is wrong, missing or not how you would say it.

  4. 4Approval

    You approve the final text in writing. Nothing goes out before that.

  5. 5Publication

    The approved work goes live: website, registry profile and press release, as your package includes.

  6. 6Delivery

    Printed pieces in your package, such as a hardcover, or a certificate and award for clients who meet eligibility, are produced from the approved text and sent to you.

  7. 7Revisions

    If a fact changes later, tell us. How many updates are included is set out in your agreement.

Questions

Does this follow attorney advertising rules?

We write to avoid the claims those rules most often restrict, such as results, comparisons and specialist titles. The rules differ by state and you remain responsible for compliance, so review the final text against your state's rules or with your bar's ethics resources before you approve it.

What do I need to send?

Your resume, bar admissions and courts, current firm details, earlier positions, any publications or talks, and a professional photo.

Do you confirm bar status?

We write from your records and public bar listings where they exist. A profile is not a bar status check. Anyone who needs that should contact the state bar.

Will clients find me on Google?

A website under your name, a registry profile and a press release give search engines more accurate pages about you. Search engines decide what they show, so we do not promise positions.

Can I mention my firm?

Yes. Your site and profile can name and link to your firm. If the firm has rules about outside publicity, check them first.

Who approves the final text?

You do. Nothing is published, printed or sent until you approve it in writing.
